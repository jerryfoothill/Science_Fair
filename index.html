Physics & Slipstream Theory

When an F1 car drives behind another, the front car punches a hole through the air, creating a low-velocity, low-density, turbulent wake zone (slipstream).

  • Drag Force reduction: Because the dynamic pressure $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ inside the wake is lower, the trailing car encounters less frontal wind resistance.
  • Distance Effect: Close distances (0m - 4m) yield maximum drag savings, but high turbulence disrupts downforce. Beyond 20m, the wake dissipates back into ambient air flow.
